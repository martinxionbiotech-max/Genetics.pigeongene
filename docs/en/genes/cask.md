# Calcium/Calmodulin-Dependent Serine Protein Kinase (CASK)

The CASK gene encodes calcium/calmodulin-dependent serine protein kinase, which takes part in synaptic function and neural development. Polymorphism research in pigeons has been published (Dybus 2023). This page covers its function, the evidence, and the limits of interpretation.

## Plain-language interpretation (an analogy)

CASK is like a **"scaffold builder"** at the synapse: it helps to build and stabilise the connecting structures between neurons. When those connections are well maintained, neural signals travel more smoothly — in pigeons, research on it has so far focused on identifying polymorphisms.

## What CASK is

The CASK (Calcium/Calmodulin-Dependent Serine Protein Kinase) gene encodes a membrane-associated guanylate kinase (MAGUK) family protein that combines kinase activity with a protein-scaffolding role, and takes part in synaptic organisation and neural development.

## Biological function

- Takes part in synapse formation and maintenance;
- Acts as a scaffold protein that organises synaptic protein complexes;
- Associated with neural development and signal transduction.

## Its role in racing pigeons

The development and function of the nervous system are fundamental to homing, learning and behavioural performance in racing pigeons. Research on CASK in pigeons is at present mainly polymorphism identification (Dybus 2023), and direct evidence linking it to racing performance is weak.

## Research evidence (evidence grade C)

- **Dybus et al. (2023, Animals)**: reported CASK gene polymorphisms in pigeons. The study itself is a polymorphism identification; the performance association is secondary content. PMID: 37443867 ｜ DOI: 10.3390/ani13132070

> Evidence grade definition: C = a single or preliminary study in pigeons.

## Genetic variation and markers

Polymorphic sites are present in pigeon CASK and can be used for research into genetic background. At present it should not be interpreted as a "racing performance locus".

## Scientific limitations

- Direct evidence of an association with racing performance is weak;
- The functional significance of the identified polymorphisms has yet to be clarified;
- A single study needs more independent verification.

## Genetic testing and breeding use

The CASK locus can serve as reference information for research into the genetic background of pigeons; it is not currently used for performance-related decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is CASK related to racing performance?**

Direct evidence of an association is weak; the existing research is mainly polymorphism identification.

**Can testing CASK predict results?**

No.

**Is CASK important in the nervous system?**

In other species CASK is associated with synaptic function, but evidence in pigeons is still limited.
