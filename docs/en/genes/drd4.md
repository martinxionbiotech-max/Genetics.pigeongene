# Dopamine Receptor D4 (DRD4)

The DRD4 gene encodes the dopamine D4 receptor and takes part in signal transmission in the nervous system. In racing pigeons, a peer-reviewed study has reported a statistical association between its polymorphism and racing performance. This page covers the gene's function, the evidence, and the limits of interpretation.

## Plain-language interpretation (an analogy)

DRD4 is like a **"signal receiver"** in the brain's reward and motivation circuits: when an environmental stimulus arrives, dopamine is the "messenger" and DRD4 is the "antenna" that receives the signal. Different antenna types may respond differently to a signal — and the research question is whether such a difference is related to racing performance.

## What DRD4 is

The DRD4 (Dopamine Receptor D4) gene encodes the dopamine D4 receptor, which belongs to the G protein-coupled receptor family. Dopamine signalling is broadly involved in neural processes such as motor control, motivation, learning and stress response. DRD4 is polymorphic in several species, and some studies examine its association with behavioural phenotypes.

## Biological function

- Mediates neuromodulatory dopamine signals;
- Associated with processes such as maintaining motivation and behavioural activation;
- Gene polymorphism may affect receptor expression or function, giving rise to individual differences.

## Its role in racing pigeons

Racing is a combined test of motivation, persistence in the right direction, and energy management. Behaviourally, a pigeon's motivation and stress state may affect its race performance; as a gene involved in neural signalling, DRD4 has been studied as a candidate gene for racing performance.

## Research evidence (evidence grade B)

- **Proskura et al. (2015, Animal Genetics)**: analysed the association between DRD4 gene polymorphism and racing performance in racing pigeons and reported a statistically significant association. PMID: 26202371 ｜ DOI: 10.1111/age.12328

> Evidence grade definition: B = a racing-pigeon-specific, peer-reviewed journal study with a statistically significant association.

## Genetic variation and markers

The research focuses on polymorphic sites in the DRD4 gene and their association with racing performance. Genotyping results should be interpreted on the basis of the phenotype definition used in the original study (racing performance) and should not be extrapolated to other traits.

## Scientific limitations

- **Association is not causation**; the dopamine system is strongly influenced by environment and training;
- "Racing performance" is a composite trait covering several dimensions, including speed, endurance, homing and motivation;
- A single study needs more independent replication.

## Genetic testing and breeding use

The DRD4 genotype can serve as reference information on a candidate locus related to behaviour and motivation. Breeding decisions are best made by combining race results, pedigree and multiple gene loci.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is DRD4 a "homing gene"?**

No. The existing research centres on racing performance; homing behaviour involves more complex polygenic and environmental factors.

**Can testing DRD4 predict results?**

No. A genotype is probabilistic reference information and does not constitute a prediction of results.

**Is the dopamine system affected by the environment?**

Yes. Training, nutrition and stress state all affect neurotransmitter systems; genes are only one background factor.
