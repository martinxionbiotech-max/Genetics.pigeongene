# Feather Keratin (F-KER)

The F-KER gene encodes feather keratin, the basic structural protein of feathers. Its polymorphism has been studied in domestic pigeons (Dybus & Haase 2011), but direct evidence linking it to racing performance is lacking. This page states the current state of the evidence honestly.

## Plain-language analogy

F-KER is like the **"building material"** of a feather: keratin is the brick that makes up the vane. Differences in the type of brick may affect the physical properties of a feather, but there are many steps between "feather material" and "flying fast", and the two cannot be equated directly.

## What F-KER is

The F-KER (Feather Keratin) gene encodes a feather keratin structural protein. Keratin is the main structural component of feathers and determines their mechanical properties and form.

## Biological function

- It is the main structural protein forming feathers;
- It affects feather strength, elasticity and form;
- Variation in related genes may affect feather mechanical characteristics (background research: Haase et al. 2022, *Scientific Reports*, reported the effect of an FCBP gene polymorphism on the Young's modulus of the tenth primary feather, PMID: 35110587 — note that FCBP and F-KER are different genes).

## Role in racing pigeons

Feather condition affects flight efficiency, which is why feather-related genes attract attention. However, research on F-KER in domestic pigeons is currently limited to polymorphism identification, and there is **no evidence of a direct association with racing performance**.

## Research evidence (evidence grade D)

- **Dybus & Haase (2011, British Poultry Science)**: reported polymorphism of the feather keratin gene (F-KER) in domestic pigeons. The study is a polymorphism identification and does not include an analysis of association with racing performance. PMID: 21491240 | DOI: 10.1080/00071668.2010.549666

> Evidence grade definition: D = biologically plausible, but racing pigeon evidence in pigeons is limited.

## Genetic variation and markers

The F-KER locus is polymorphic in domestic pigeons and can be used for breed genetic research. It should not be promoted as a "flight performance gene" locus.

## Scientific limitations

- Direct evidence of an association with racing performance is missing;
- There is no direct chain of evidence between "feather structure" and "racing results";
- The functional significance of the polymorphism has yet to be clarified.

## Genetic testing and breeding applications

The F-KER locus can serve as reference information for research into the genetic background of feather traits; it is not currently used for performance-related decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is F-KER a "flight performance gene"?**

The available evidence does not support that description. Research has been limited to polymorphism identification.

**Are feathers related to flight?**

Feather mechanics affect flight efficiency, but direct evidence linking F-KER to racing results is lacking.

**Is testing for F-KER useful?**

It can be used for genetic background research, but it should not be used to predict performance.
