# Cryptochrome 1 (CRY1)

The CRY1 gene encodes cryptochrome 1, a blue-light-sensing protein that takes part in regulating the biological circadian rhythm. In homing pigeons, research has reported a statistical association between its polymorphism and racing performance. This page covers its function, the evidence, and the limits of interpretation.

## Plain-language interpretation (an analogy)

CRY1 is like a **"blue-light sensing clock"** inside the cell: it helps the body know what time it is and whether it is day or night. Racing pigeons fly long distances across day and night, and the ability to regulate rhythm may be related to flight condition — but "light-based timing" and a "magnetic compass" are two different things that should not be conflated.

## What CRY1 is

The CRY1 (Cryptochrome 1) gene encodes the cryptochrome 1 protein, which belongs to the blue-light photoreceptor family. In mammals and birds, cryptochromes take part in regulating the core circadian circuit and form part of the biological clock.

## Biological function

- Senses blue-light signals and takes part in synchronising circadian rhythm;
- Acts as a negative regulatory element of the biological clock in rhythm oscillation;
- Associated with the circadian organisation of sleep, metabolism and behaviour.

## Magnetic sensing: distinguishing CRY1 from CRY4

Molecular candidate research into magnetoreception in birds focuses more on **CRY4** (Hochstoeger et al. 2020, *Science Advances*, a molecular and anatomical study of pigeon CRY4; PMID: 32851187). CRY1 and CRY4 are different cryptochrome members; the racing performance association study discussed on this page concerns CRY1, and it cannot be used to claim that CRY1 is a "magnetoreception gene".

## Research evidence (evidence grade B)

- **Dybus et al. (2021, Animals)**: analysed the association between CRY1 gene polymorphism and racing performance in homing pigeons and reported a statistically significant association. PMID: 34573598 ｜ DOI: 10.3390/ani11092632

> Evidence grade definition: B = a racing-pigeon-specific, peer-reviewed journal study with a statistically significant association.

## Genetic variation and markers

The research focuses on polymorphic sites in the CRY1 gene. Genotyping results should be interpreted within the trait definition used in the original study (racing performance).

## Scientific limitations

- **Association is not causation**;
- The molecular mechanism of magnetoreception in birds is still an active research field, and the role of CRY1 has not been clarified;
- A single primary study needs independent replication.

## Genetic testing and breeding use

The CRY1 genotype can serve as reference information on a candidate locus related to rhythm and racing performance, to be used together with other loci and with race results.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is CRY1 a "magnetoreception gene"?**

Not at present. Candidate magnetoreception research focuses more on CRY4; the evidence for CRY1 concerns a racing performance association, and the two should not be conflated.

**Can testing CRY1 predict results?**

No. A genotype is probabilistic reference information.

**Does circadian rhythm matter for racing pigeons?**

Long-distance racing crosses day and night, and rhythm regulation is thought to possibly affect flight condition, but the specific mechanism still needs research.
