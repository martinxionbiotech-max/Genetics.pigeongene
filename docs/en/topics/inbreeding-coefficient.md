# Inbreeding and Inbreeding Coefficient

The inbreeding coefficient (F) measures the probability that an individual's two alleles are "identical by descent" (inherited as the same copy from a common ancestor). It is the core tool breeders use to assess the degree of inbreeding.

## Plain-language view (an analogy)

The inbreeding coefficient is like a **"bloodline overlap score"**: 0 means the parents share no common ancestor (fully outcrossed), and the higher the value, the more closely related the parents are. Inbreeding is a "double-edged sword" — it fixes the strengths while also concentrating and exposing recessive defects.

## What the inbreeding coefficient means

- **F = 0**: no common ancestor (idealised outcrossing);
- **F = 0.25**: equivalent to a parent-offspring or full-sibling mating;
- **The larger F is**: the more common ancestors the parents share, and the closer the relationship.

The calculation is based on path analysis of the common ancestors in the pedigree, with the formula F = Σ(1/2)^(n₁+n₂+1), where n₁ and n₂ are the numbers of generations from each parent to the common ancestor.

## The two sides of inbreeding

**Advantages**:
- Quickly fixes favourable traits and improves uniformity;
- Concentrates the genetic contribution of outstanding ancestors.

**Risks (inbreeding depression)**:
- Harmful recessive alleles become homozygous, exposing genetic defects;
- Vigour, fertility and disease resistance decline;
- Performance and uniformity of body type decline.

## Practical recommendations

- Keep complete pedigree records and assess the inbreeding coefficient of a pairing regularly;
- Alternate inbreeding with outcrossing ("inbreed to fix, outcross to revitalise");
- Strengthen health monitoring in the offspring of highly inbred lines and introduce outside blood in good time;
- Keep several family lines so that the population does not narrow to a single line.

## Common misconceptions

- "A high inbreeding coefficient means the pigeon is bound to be poor" — a high F raises risk, but does not mean the individual is necessarily defective;
- "Outcrossing is absolutely safe" — outcrossing introduces new variation, which carries its own uncertainty;
- "Judge how closely related birds are by feel" — rely on the pedigree calculation, not on impression.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is the inbreeding coefficient calculated?**

It is based on path analysis of the pedigree and requires complete ancestry records; dedicated breeding software can also assist with the calculation.

**What counts as "too close"?**

There is no single threshold. Tolerance differs between lines, so it has to be assessed dynamically against the offspring's vigour and race results.

**Can inbreeding produce good pigeons?**

Yes. Many famous lines have fixed traits through inbreeding, but it needs to be combined with strict selection and timely outcrossing.
